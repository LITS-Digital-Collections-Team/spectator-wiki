William Wirt Howe

Overview

William Wirt Howe (1833–1909) was a Hamilton College alumnus (Class of 1853) who became a prominent jurist and legal scholar, serving as a Federal judge in New Orleans and authoring works on civil law and Louisiana legal history. Named for the celebrated orator William Wirt, he pursued a legal career in the South after the Civil War and was recognized by Hamilton with an honorary LL.D. He is notable in the Hamilton corpus as a recurring figure in alumni listings spanning five decades of course catalogs, and his name appears consistently among the college’s most distinguished 19th-century graduates.

Relevance to Research

Howe appears in Hamilton course catalogs from his student years (1850–53) through the early 20th century, documenting his trajectory from enrolled student to honored alumnus. He is listed as a sophomore in the 1850–51 catalog (under his class grouping), as a junior in 1851–52, and as a senior in 1852–53 with the residence Canandaigua, room 28D. By the 1881–82 catalog he appears in the alumni publications list as the author of “The Pasha Papers: Epistles of Mohammed Pasha” (published in New York). By 1902–03 he is listed among the college’s half-century annalists for the Class of 1853, with the title “Hon. William Wirt Howe, LL.D., New Orleans, La.” — confirming his honorary degree and his residence in New Orleans. The Hamilton Life and later Spectator corpus contains no further substantive mentions; the 1977 Spectator reference to a “William Howe” is to the British general of the Revolutionary War era, not this alumnus.
