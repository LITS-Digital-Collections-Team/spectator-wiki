Alpha Delta Phi

Overview

Alpha Delta Phi (ΑΔΦ) is a Greek-letter social fraternity and literary society founded at Hamilton College in January 1832 by Samuel Eells and four other students, making it the oldest surviving Greek organization with roots at Hamilton. Wikipedia confirms the 1832 founding date at Hamilton. Its founding predates the Spectator corpus by over a century, but the chapter’s presence is documented throughout the archive, particularly in connection with the major institutional changes to Greek life in the 1990s and early 2000s.

Relevance to Research

Alpha Delta Phi appears in the Spectator corpus primarily in the context of disciplinary actions tied to Hamilton’s alcohol reform era. President Eugene Tobin suspended the chapter’s recognition in February 2000 following a Mardi Gras party that sent multiple students to the emergency room — one of the more serious documented alcohol incidents in the 1996–2003 period. The chapter was subsequently reinstated by Dean of Students Flossie Mitchell on October 28, 2003, documented in the November 7, 2003 Spectator. The chapter also lost exclusive control of its house under the 1995 Residential Life Decision, which required all Greek societies to integrate their houses into the college housing lottery. The chapter’s founding in 1832 is noted in the Wikipedia-sourced material as the earliest documented Greek founding at Hamilton.

Notes

Type: Fraternity (Greek-letter organization), retains literary society identity
Active period at Hamilton: Founded January 1832; ongoing (with documented suspension 2000–2003)
Colors: Emerald green and pearl white
Motto: Manus Multae Cor Unum (“Many Hands, One Heart”)

Founding at Hamilton (January 1832): Samuel Eells arrived at Hamilton and found two existing literary societies — the Phoenix and the Philopeuthian — whose recruiting rivalry he found distasteful. He proposed a new society based on “the loftiest of intellectual and moral ideals” with members drawn from both. In January 1832, Eells gathered four others in his room to found the fraternity: Lorenzo Latham, John Curtiss Underwood, Oliver Andrew Morse, and Henry Lemuel Storrs. Eells wrote the constitution at that first meeting; he and Latham designed the emblem and symbols.

Oliver Andrew Morse is one of the five original co-founders and appears in the Hamilton Notable Alumni list. He was a member of the class of 1833.

National expansion: In 1832, Alpha Delta Phi became the first fraternity to establish a chapter west of the Appalachian Mountains (at Miami University), preceding the formation of the Miami Triad. It expanded to Yale, Harvard (1836, later disaffiliated to form the A.D. Club), Dartmouth (1846–1969, inspiration for National Lampoon’s Animal House), University of Michigan (1846), Cornell, and many others. Alpha Delta Phi is a charter member of the North American Interfraternity Conference (NIC).

Hamilton chapter standing: According to the Almanac of Fraternities and Sororities, the Alpha chapter at Hamilton is the third oldest continuously operating chapter in the North American Fraternity System and the second oldest Alpha chapter.
