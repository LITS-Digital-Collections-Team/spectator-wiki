David Aldrich Nelson

Overview

David Aldrich Nelson was a Hamilton College student of the Class of 1954. He later pursued a legal career and served as a federal judge on the United States Court of Appeals for the Sixth Circuit. His corpus appearances as a student capture early 1950s undergraduate life at Hamilton, including student engagement with scholarship programs and the honor system.

Relevance to Research

Nelson appears in The Spectator corpus in 1950 and 1951 as a current student. In October 1950, he is listed among 23 freshmen awarded New York State University Scholarships, bringing the total number of NYS scholarship holders at Hamilton to 74. In February 1951, as a sophomore, he co-signed a letter to the editor — “Ralph Schoenstein, ‘53; Charles Levy, ‘53; David Nelson, ‘54” — protesting the Political Science 2 department’s policy of scheduling an evening exam during finals week, arguing that the practice undermined the student honor system. These appearances document the ordinary rhythms of postwar student life: scholarship administration, academic advocacy, and peer-to-peer correspondence in the student newspaper.
