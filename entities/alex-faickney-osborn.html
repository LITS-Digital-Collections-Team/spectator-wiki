Alex Faickney Osborn

Overview

Alex Faickney Osborn (1888–1966) was an American advertising executive, author, and creativity theorist best known for coining the term “brainstorming” and developing the brainstorming technique for group creative problem-solving. He co-founded the advertising agency Batten, Barton, Durstine & Osborn (BBDO) in 1928, one of the most influential agencies in American advertising history. Osborn graduated from Hamilton College with the Class of 1909 and served as editor-in-chief of Hamilton Life in his senior year (1908–09).

Relevance to Research

Osborn’s Hamilton Life editorship in 1908–09 places him at the founding moment of the 1908–1942 Hamilton Life corpus — he was EIC in the first year of the archive’s second major run. His subsequent career in advertising and creativity theory makes him one of Hamilton’s most commercially and intellectually influential alumni of the 20th century. The term “brainstorming” — which he coined in Applied Imagination (1953) — entered the global lexicon from a Hamilton alumnus.
