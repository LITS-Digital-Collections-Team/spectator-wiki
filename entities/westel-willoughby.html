Overview

Westel Willoughby (1830–c.1898) was a Hamilton alumnus (class of 1854) who became a distinguished lawyer and jurist. Born in Groton, New York, he entered Hamilton as a sophomore in 1851 and graduated with the highest academic rank. He served as a Union Army officer in the Civil War, was wounded at Chancellorsville, then built a distinguished legal career in Virginia, rising successively to Commonwealth’s Attorney, Circuit Judge, and member of the Virginia Supreme Court of Appeals. He later resided in Washington, D.C.

Relevance to Research

The 1852–53 and 1853–54 catalogs list “Westel Willoughby” as a student. The 1893–94 catalog’s obituary section for Benjamin Franklin Willoughby (class of 1856) identifies Westel Willoughby ‘54 as Benjamin’s brother, residing in Washington, D.C. The 1898–99 catalog carries a full memorial notice for the class of 1854, giving his biography: born April 3, 1830, in Groton, N.Y.; entered the sophomore class in 1851; graduated with highest honors; practiced law in Groton; served as Major of the 137th Regiment N.Y. Volunteers; wounded at Chancellorsville; appointed Commonwealth’s Attorney, Circuit Judge, and member of the Supreme Court of Appeals of Virginia; spent his final years in Washington.

Notes

Westel Willoughby ‘54 is a distinct individual from his brother Benjamin Franklin Willoughby ‘56, who also attended Hamilton. The 1893–94 catalog bio for Benjamin explicitly names Westel as his brother. The full memorial in the 1898–99 catalog confirms his death sometime before that catalog’s publication.
