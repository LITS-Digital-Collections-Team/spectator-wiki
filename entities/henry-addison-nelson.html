Henry Addison Nelson

Overview

Henry Addison Nelson (1820–1906) was a Hamilton College alumnus (Class of 1840) who became a prominent Presbyterian minister and biblical scholar. He served for many years in St. Louis, Missouri, and later in Wooster, Ohio, and was regarded as a distinguished churchman within the Presbyterian denomination. He was awarded the honorary degree of Doctor of Divinity (D.D.) by Hamilton, reflecting his standing in American religious life.

Nelson died in 1906, and the 1907–08 catalog records his death in the obituary section. He had a long career that spanned more than six decades after his Hamilton graduation, and he maintained his connection to the college throughout.

Relevance to Research

Nelson exemplifies the antebellum Hamilton tradition of producing Presbyterian ministers of national prominence. His appearance across six decades of Hamilton catalogs — from the 1857–58 catalog (as a living distinguished alumnus) through the 1907–08 obituary — provides a longitudinal view of how the college tracked and commemorated its alumni clergy. In the 1864–65 catalog, he is listed as the Class of 1840 orator for the Alumni Day program, indicating his status as a representative voice of his era. His D.D. degree and long ministry reflect Hamilton’s deep ties to Presbyterian institutional life in the nineteenth century.
