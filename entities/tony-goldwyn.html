Overview

Tony Goldwyn (born May 20, 1960) is an American actor and director best known for playing the villain Carter Burke in Ghost (1990) and for his lead role as President Fitzgerald Grant in the ABC drama Scandal (2012–2018). He is the grandson of MGM co-founder Samuel Goldwyn. Goldwyn graduated from Hamilton College with the Class of 1982 and was an active and prominent presence in Hamilton’s theater program during the late 1970s and early 1980s. After Hamilton he trained at the London Academy of Music and Dramatic Art (LAMDA) before launching his professional career.

Relevance to Research

Goldwyn’s undergraduate acting career at Hamilton is well documented in the Spectator. He appeared in at least four productions between spring 1979 and spring 1980, demonstrating a rapid rise from ensemble roles to leads. In spring 1979 he had a role in Now I Lay Me Down to Sleep, a senior project written and directed by Tracy Shoolman. In fall 1979 he played Valere (the young lover) in Moliere’s Tartuffe, and the Spectator reviewer singled him out as “definitely the more dynamic actor.” In spring 1980, as a sophomore, he took the title role (Brother Jerome Savonarola) in Armand Salacrou’s The World Is Round directed by assistant professor Rod Umlas, a significant lead for an underclassman. A student reviewer described watching Goldwyn be flogged onstage in that production with visceral discomfort. He also appeared in another production in May 1979. These notices establish him as one of the most accomplished student actors of his Hamilton cohort.
