Overview

Edward Skinner King (1861–1931) was a Hamilton College alumnus of the Class of 1888 who became a prominent astronomer at Harvard College Observatory. Born in Liverpool, New York, he enrolled at Hamilton in the early 1880s and graduated in 1888. He spent his career at Harvard, where he worked in astrophotography and stellar photometry, earning recognition as an authority in observational astronomy.

Hamilton awarded King an honorary Doctor of Science degree in 1927, acknowledging his distinguished career at Harvard. He appears in the 1927-28 catalog as a recipient of that degree, listed as Edward Skinner King of Cambridge, Massachusetts — the city where Harvard Observatory is located.

Relevance to Research

King represents the tradition of Hamilton alumni achieving national distinction in science. His presence in the student catalogs of the 1880s documents his undergraduate years at Hamilton, and his honorary degree in 1927 marks the college’s formal recognition of his career in astronomy. The Hamilton Life retrospective reference in 1932 confirms continued alumni memory of King after his death in 1931.
