John Norton Pomeroy

Overview

John Norton Pomeroy (1828–1885) was a Hamilton College alumnus (Class of ~1847) who became one of the most prominent American legal scholars of the nineteenth century. He is best known for his treatise “A Treatise on Equity Jurisprudence,” a foundational work in American equity law. He was a professor at Hastings College of the Law in San Francisco and authored several influential legal texts during his career.

The 1865–66 Hamilton catalog lists him as a faculty member or honorary degree recipient, reflecting his early prominence. He died in 1885, and later catalogs reference a Theodore M. Pomeroy Loan Fund (a different Pomeroy family member, Theodore Medad Pomeroy, Class of 1842), indicating the Pomeroy name’s continued resonance at Hamilton.

Relevance to Research

Pomeroy represents Hamilton’s tradition of producing legal scholars of national stature. His presence in the 1865–66 catalog — where he appears alongside faculty members with honorary LL.D. degrees — reflects his early national reputation. His distinction as author of “A Treatise on Equity Jurisprudence” made him a frequently cited authority in American courts throughout the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries.

Notes
